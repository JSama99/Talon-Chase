Safeguards

What it will never do.

A machine that emails your carriers has to be boring and careful. These rules are in the code, and each one has an automated test that fails if it is broken.

  1. Write to an address you do not know

    It writes only to contacts on your carrier record. A new address that shows up in a reply is not used until one of your people approves it.

  2. Answer a message about money

    Bank details, remit-to, factoring, wire, ACH, a notice of assignment: no reply is sent. The message is held and goes to a person, attachment and all.

  3. File a POD with a note as clean

    Shortage, damage, refusal or a seal note on the POD always goes to a person, whatever else is right with the document.

  4. Tell a carrier more than the load number

    A request carries the load number and nothing else about the load. A guard refuses any message containing the rate, the pickup number, or your customer’s or consignee’s name.

  5. Send at night

    Nothing goes out between 8 pm and 7 am in the carrier’s own time zone. A load delivered at 9 pm is asked about at 7 the next morning.

  6. Ask a fourth time

    Three requests, then it stops and hands you the load with the carrier’s phone numbers and the full history.

  7. Text someone who has not agreed

    Texts need consent on file. STOP means stop, and only written consent recorded by one of your people turns texts back on.

  8. Keep sending after you say stop

    One switch stops every message for your brokerage. Anyone on your team can turn it off. Queued messages are held, not lost.

Your side

Two minutes a day.

Open it, clear a short list, leave. Each row says what happened in plain words and gives you the carrier’s phone number. Open a load to see every message, the document, and what was done about it.

No passwords. You sign in with a link sent to your email.

The record

When someone asks “did you ever get it?”

Every request, reply, document, check and filing is added to a chain for that load. Each entry is sealed with a fingerprint of the one before it, so an entry changed later breaks the chain and the check says where.

You can export the record for any load and check it yourself, with a small script that needs nothing from us.

Where it stands

What works today, and what does not.

You should know exactly what you would be trying.

Built Working now

  • The chase itselfThe schedule, quiet hours, weekends, three requests, the hand-off to a person, and every safeguard above.
  • Email, both waysRequests go out and replies, attachments and bounces come back in, through Resend.
  • A carrier upload pageOne link, one button, made for a driver’s phone. It shows the load number and nothing else.
  • Your screensThe list of what needs you, the story of each load, settings, and the off switch.
  • The recordThe chain for every load, the export, and the script that checks it.

Not yet Not built

  • Reading the PODA reader that uses a Claude model is built, but it has not read a real POD yet. Until it has been measured on real ones, every document that arrives comes to you to look at.
  • Filing into your TMSThere is no TMS connection yet. What exists is an emailed packet, which works with any TMS.
  • Text messagesThe consent rules are built. No text line is connected, so the texts in the recordings above went to a stand-in.
  • Public holidaysThe schedule knows weekends and quiet hours. It does not know holidays.
